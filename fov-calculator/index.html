What Is Field of View (FOV)?

Field of view (FOV) is the extent of the observable world seen at any given moment. In gaming, FOV determines how much of the game world is visible on screen. It is measured in degrees and calculated based on your monitor's physical width and how far you sit from it.

The formula is: FOV = 2 x atan(monitor width / (2 x viewing distance)) x (180 / PI)

Setting your in-game FOV to match your physical FOV creates the most natural and immersive experience, reducing motion sickness and eye strain.

FOV Classifications

  • Too Narrow (below 60 degrees) — Tunnel vision effect. You miss peripheral information. Common when sitting too far from a small monitor.
  • Narrow (60-80 degrees) — Limited peripheral vision. Acceptable for casual gaming but not ideal for competitive play.
  • Natural (80-100 degrees) — Comfortable and immersive. Matches natural human peripheral focus for most desk setups.
  • Wide (100-120 degrees) — Extended peripheral vision. Great for competitive FPS games where awareness matters.
  • Ultra-wide (above 120 degrees) — Very wide view, typical of ultra-wide monitors at close distance. Can cause fish-eye distortion in some games.

Frequently Asked Questions

What FOV should I use for gaming?

The ideal FOV depends on your monitor size and how far you sit from the screen. A natural FOV of 80-100 degrees is comfortable for most setups. For competitive FPS games, many players prefer 90-110 degrees for a wider view. Use this calculator to find your physically correct FOV.

Does a higher FOV reduce FPS?

Yes, a higher FOV means the game engine renders more of the scene, which can slightly reduce FPS. The impact is usually small (2-5%) but can be larger in open-world games. Competitive players often accept this trade-off for better spatial awareness.

Why does a wrong FOV cause motion sickness?

If your in-game FOV does not match the actual angular size of your monitor from your viewing position, your eyes and inner ear send conflicting signals. A FOV that is too low creates a tunnel-vision effect, while a FOV that is too high creates a fish-eye distortion. Both can cause motion sickness.
